## Purpose

Most routine case-status and policy questions can be answered directly
from case data and this knowledge base. A defined set of situations
should always be routed to a human specialist instead of answered by
the agent, because they involve money, legal exposure, emotional
sensitivity, or a judgment call outside published policy. This document
is the master list; other documents point back here for specifics.

## Hard escalation triggers

The following must always be escalated to a human specialist and never
resolved, adjudicated, or promised by the agent:

1. **Billing disputes** — disputed charges, invoice disagreements,
   duplicate-charge claims, or any refund amount/approval
   (`billing-and-payment-terms.md`, `refund-policy.md`).
2. **Legal or law-enforcement records requests**, including subpoenas,
   court orders, or attorney inquiries about a case.
3. **Media inquiries** of any kind about a case or about Meridian.
4. **Complaints or dissatisfaction** with how a case was handled,
   including disputes about report findings
   (`results-delivery-and-formats.md`).
5. **Identity-verification overrides** — anyone who cannot be matched
   to the case's authorized contacts but insists they should have
   access (`case-information-authorization.md`).
6. **Early data-deletion requests** or retention-extension requests
   beyond 12 months (`privacy-and-data-retention.md`).
7. **Total sample/sequencing failure** with no viable path forward
   (`sample-failure-and-recourse.md`).
8. **Signs of emotional distress or crisis** in a requester's message —
   these cases involve identification of human remains and can surface
   grief or trauma that warrants a specialist, not a bot, on the other
   end.
9. Any request that falls outside the scope of the policies in this
   knowledge base — when in doubt, escalate rather than guess.

## What "escalate" means in practice

Acknowledge the request, state plainly that it's being routed to a
specialist who will follow up, and do not speculate about the outcome
(a dollar amount, a legal answer, a revised date, or an identification
determination). Escalating is not a failure state — it is the correct,
expected outcome for these categories.

## What does not need escalation

Routine status questions, stage and turnaround explanations, the
always-granted requests listed in `case-information-authorization.md`
(adding an authorized contact, resending a delivered report, extending
retention up to 12 months), and general policy questions answerable
from this knowledge base can all be handled directly — escalation
exists for the specific categories above, not as a default for
uncertainty about case facts (which should instead be answered from
the case record or, if truly unavailable, stated as unavailable).
