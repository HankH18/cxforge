## Two kinds of submissions

Meridian Forensic Genomics accepts two categories of material:

1. **Evidentiary samples** — skeletal remains, teeth, or tissue
   associated with an unidentified-person or cold-case investigation.
   These are submitted by the law-enforcement agency, medical
   examiner, or coroner's office that has custody of the case, not by
   members of the public.
2. **Reference (comparison) samples** — buccal (cheek swab) samples
   from living relatives, used to compare against a case profile
   during genealogy research. These are submitted by family members
   using a reference kit mailed from our intake team.

## Submission steps

1. Contact intake to open a case and receive a case ID and, for
   evidentiary samples, a chain-of-custody (CoC) form; for reference
   kits, a prepaid mailer with the swab kit and instructions.
2. Complete the CoC form (evidentiary) or the consent and identity
   section of the reference kit (family submissions). Both require a
   signature.
3. Package the sample per the enclosed instructions — evidentiary
   material must remain in its original secure packaging with an
   unbroken seal; reference swabs must be fully air-dried before
   sealing to prevent degradation.
4. Ship using the provided tracked shipping label. Do not ship
   perishable or wet material.

## Chain-of-custody requirements

For every evidentiary sample, chain-of-custody documentation must show
an unbroken chain from point of recovery to receipt at our facility:
who collected it, when, how it was stored, and every transfer of
custody in between. Our intake team verifies this documentation before
the case can proceed past intake — a gap in the chain delays intake
until the submitting agency supplies the missing link, since testimony
about the sample's origin depends on it. Reference samples from family
members do not require a forensic CoC, only signed consent and
identity confirmation, since they are used for comparison rather than
as case evidence.

## Sample handling on arrival

On arrival, every sample is logged, photographed (see
`dna-profiles-and-genealogy-research.md` for how photos are used
downstream), and assigned to intake. `photos_available` on a case
reflects whether accession photography has been completed; it is
common for this to be `false` briefly for a just-arrived sample before
the intake team finishes logging it.

## Questions about a specific submission

Requesters asking whether their sample has arrived or whether their
paperwork is complete should be pointed to their case's current stage
(see `pipeline-stages-overview.md`) — a case still showing `intake`
with an old `stage_entered_at` date more often indicates missing or
incomplete chain-of-custody paperwork than a lost sample.
