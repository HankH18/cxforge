## Why genealogy outcomes are not guaranteed

Unlike extraction and sequencing, which are lab processes we control
end to end, genealogy research depends on whether the case subject's
genetic relatives happen to be present in opt-in genetic genealogy
databases — something outside our control. A technically excellent DNA
profile can still produce a weak or no genealogy lead if the case
subject's family has little presence in those databases. This should
always be explained to requesters before genealogy research begins,
and revisited if the case's genealogy phase is taking a long time or
appears to conclude without a strong lead.

## What "no result" means

If genealogy research concludes without identifying a strong candidate
lead, that is a valid, completed outcome — not a failure of the work.
The genealogy team still documents which matches were found, how
distant they were, and why they weren't sufficient to build a
confident lead, and this goes into the final report per
`results-delivery-and-formats.md`. A case can be `complete` with a
delivered report and still not have resolved the case subject's
identity.

## Why timelines vary so widely

The 3–12 week genealogy window in `turnaround-times.md` is wide because
match density varies enormously: a case with several close-relative
matches can be triangulated in a few weeks, while a case with only
distant matches (third cousins or further) can require building out
large, time-consuming family trees before a candidate emerges, if one
does at all.

## Leads are not identifications

As covered in `dna-profiles-and-genealogy-research.md`, any candidate
identity from genealogy research is an investigative lead requiring
confirmatory testing before it can be treated as a legal
identification. Never tell a requester their case has been "solved" or
their relative "identified" based on a genealogy lead alone — describe
it as a candidate lead provided to the submitting agency.

## Setting expectations at intake and mid-case

When a requester asks what to expect from genealogy, or asks why it's
taking so long compared to extraction or sequencing, explain the
database-dependency point above rather than implying we control the
outcome the way we control lab turnaround. If a requester is
distressed about an inconclusive or slow genealogy outcome, that
emotional-support conversation is a specialist matter — see
`escalation-and-specialist-requests.md`.
