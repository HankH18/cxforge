## Which stages can be rushed

Rush processing is available for the **extraction** and **sequencing**
stages only. Intake is administrative and already short; genealogy
depends on external database match density rather than lab throughput,
so paying for a rush cannot make relative matches appear faster — see
`genealogy-limitations-and-expectations.md`.

## Rush pricing and floors

Rush processing carries a **50% surcharge** on the fee for the stage
being rushed (see `billing-and-payment-terms.md` for the base fee
structure). A rush shortens the stage's expected window but cannot
bypass lab-physical minimums:

- **Extraction rush floor: 2 weeks** (standard window: 3–5 weeks).
- **Sequencing rush floor: 3 weeks** (standard window: 3–8 weeks,
  typically 4–6).

If a case is already tracking toward the low end of its stage's
standard window, a rush surcharge may not meaningfully change the
`eta_weeks` — say so plainly rather than promising a specific sped-up
date.

## How to request one

A rush request should be submitted by the case's primary requester or
an authorized contact and specifies which upcoming stage to rush. Rush
requests are subject to **current lab capacity** — during high case
volume, rush slots for a given week may be full even for a requester
willing to pay the surcharge.

## Is a rush request always granted?

No — unlike the always-granted list in
`case-information-authorization.md`, a rush request is a paid service
change that depends on current lab capacity and is not guaranteed. The
agent can explain eligibility, pricing, and floors from this document,
and can log the request, but confirming a rush slot is available and
processing the surcharge should go to a specialist who can check
current capacity before committing to a shortened date.

## Cases already in genealogy or complete

Rush requests do not apply retroactively to a stage a case has already
passed, and complete cases have no remaining stage to rush. A
requester asking to "rush my whole case" after it has left extraction
should be told which stage, if any, is still eligible.
